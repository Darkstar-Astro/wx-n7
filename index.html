Method & assumptions

Data. Open-Meteo, fetched live from your browser, one request per model: ECMWF IFS, DWD ICON (seamless: D2→EU→global), Météo-France (AROME→ARPEGE), KNMI Harmonie, UK Met Office, NOAA GFS. Aerosol optical depth comes from CAMS (Open-Meteo air-quality API). A model that is out of domain or returns nothing is left out, and the top-right list shows which ones loaded.

Consensus. Each metric is the median across the models that loaded, except high cloud (worst model, i.e. maximum) (worst model, i.e. maximum), because models routinely miss or mistime thin cirrus. Transparency is the median across models, each one calculated with that worst-case high cloud. Model agreement is the standard deviation of total cloud cover across models.

Seeing (a proxy, not a measurement). None of these models outputs seeing. It is estimated per model as
arcsec = 0.9 + 0.045·max(0, jet−12) + 0.04·max(0, shear−8) + 0.06·max(0, gust−4)
with jet = max(wind 200 hPa, 250 hPa), shear = |wind 500 − wind 850 hPa| and gust the 10 m gust, all in m/s. It is calibrated loosely to Benelux experience (jet stream above ~30 m/s ≈ poor seeing). It does not account for local ground-layer or tube/mirror effects. Use it for relative ranking between nights, not as an absolute FWHM.

Seeing scale. 5 Excellent (<1.2″), 4 Good (1.2–1.7″), 3 Average (1.7–2.3″), 2 Poor (2.3–3″), 1 Bad (>3″). The arcsecond value is the estimated star blur. Typical good nights in Belgium are 3–4.

Transparency (0–100). 100 − 0.6·high cloud − 1.2·max(0, RH₂ₘ−70) − 0.3·max(0, RH₇₀₀−60) − 80·AOD. High cloud is penalised because thin cirrus ruins transparency even when the total cloud figure looks low. Low and mid cloud already count in the cloud rows.

Confidence per night: high within ~30 h, medium up to ~54 h, low beyond that. It drops a step when the models disagree on cloud (spread >12%), two steps when they disagree strongly (>25%), and one step if fewer than 3 models loaded. "Partial" means the forecast data ends during that night.

Fog. Uses the model visibility where a model gives it (under 1 km is high risk, under 2 km elevated). Otherwise, a temperature − dew point spread of 0.5 °C or less with wind under 3 m/s counts as high risk. The page shows the median across models.

Transparency scale. 5 Excellent (≥80), 4 Good (65–79), 3 Average (50–64), 2 Poor (35–49), 1 Bad (<35), from the 0–100 formula above.

Dew. Temperature − dew point at 2 m: under 1.5 °C means high dew risk, under 3 °C moderate.

Score (0–100). 0.65·(100−cloud) + 0.2·transparency + 0.15·seeing (scaled), capped at 20 when cloud is over 70%. The moon does not count toward the score (it matters for DSOs but not for planets). It shows in the Darkness row.

SQM (estimate). No model forecasts sky brightness. It starts from the location's baseline SQM (clear, moonless, astro-dark zenith), which you set with ✎. Defaults: Aalst 19.0 (measured median, Unihedron SQM). Lesse (Redu) 21.1 and Grandpré 21.5 are derived from lightpollutionmap.app (Bortle 4.4 and 3.6), so replace them with your SQM readings. Replace them with your own meter reading or the World Atlas value from lightpollutionmap.info. The baseline is split into natural sky (21.9) and artificial light. Light pollution is scaled up by humidity above 70%, by AOD above 0.1, and by cloud (×(1+3·cloud), because clouds reflect city light back down). Natural sky is dimmed by cloud. Twilight adds 21.9−0.82·(sun alt+18) mag above −18°. The moon adds a term that reaches about 18.1 mag/arcsec² for a full moon at 60° altitude, scaled by the standard phase law and by sin(alt)0.8, and boosted by aerosols. Expect ±0.3–0.5 mag. "SQM if clear" shows what the sky would read with zero cloud, which is useful when the clouds are uncertain.

Bortle is converted from SQM with the common approximate table: ≥21.99 → 1, 21.89 → 2, 21.69 → 3, 20.49 → 4, 19.50 → 5, 18.94 → 6, 18.38 → 7, 17.80 → 8, below that 9. Bortle is really a visual scale, so this is a rough equivalent. The hourly row shows how much the moon, twilight and cloud degrade the site that night.

Sun and moon. Computed locally from low-precision ephemerides (about 0.5° accuracy, with a topocentric moon-parallax correction). Darkness row colours run from day to civil, nautical and astronomical twilight to astro-dark. Grey means the moon is up, and a brighter grey means a brighter moon.

Times are local to the selected location.
